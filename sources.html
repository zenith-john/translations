Sources and reuse

Each reader identifies its source editions. The terms below apply to the corresponding transcriptions, dictionary data, and fonts.

Sanskrit and Tibetan sources

GRETIL transcriptions are included under their stated noncommercial share-alike terms. These readers present the ancient text alongside independently prepared Chinese drafts and word cards. GRETIL · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

The Sanskrit edition of Madhyamakāvatāra uses Lasic, Li, MacDonald and colleagues for chapters 1–5 and Li for chapter 6. Both editions are available under Creative Commons Attribution terms. The reader extracts the root verses, groups them for comparison, and adds word cards. Chapters 1–5 · Chapter 6

Several Tibetan texts use Esukhia’s public-domain Derge transcriptions. Sanskrit lexical and morphological references include the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit and Sanskrit Heritage. Tibetan word cards provide Chinese glosses and retain source links. Derge Tengyur · Derge Kangyur · DCS · CC BY 4.0 · Sanskrit Heritage

The Lotus Sūtra and Diamond Sūtra are Chinese reading drafts with links to their source editions. The Mūlamadhyamakakārikā includes Kumārajīva’s classical Chinese. Lotus Sūtra source · Diamond Sūtra source

The Aṣṭādhyāyī reader credits Nīlesh and the contributors to ashtadhyayi.com for the source data and includes S. C. Vasu’s historical English translation. Ashtadhyayi source data and reuse terms

Classical Japanese sources

The Japanese readers present the classical originals, historical notes where included, and bilingual word cards. The Genji edition credits Shibuya Eiichi and Wikisource under CC BY-SA 3.0. The Pillow Book follows the Kokubun Taikan text on Wikisource. Genji source · Pillow Book source · CC BY-SA 3.0

Dictionary excerpts credit JMdict and Tomoshi and follow their applicable share-alike terms. The readers identify where the glosses were shortened or adapted. JMdict license · Tomoshi data · CC BY-SA 4.0

Fonts

The standalone readers embed Noto font subsets under the SIL Open Font License and carry the notices in their HTML. SIL Open Font License
